What the intensive care unit provides

The ICU is equipped for patients who need continuous observation and organ support rather than a general ward bed. Every bed has piped oxygen and suction at the bedside, so support does not depend on moving cylinders between rooms.

  • Ventilator support for patients unable to breathe adequately on their own
  • Central oxygen and central suction piped to each bed
  • Cardiac monitoring, with ECG, echocardiography and Echo Doppler available on site
  • X-ray on the premises, so imaging does not require transferring the patient
  • An in-house medical store, so drugs are available without sending relatives out at night

The unit sits inside an 18-bed hospital, so patients step down from intensive care to a ward bed in the same building, under the same team.

Conditions managed in intensive care

The ICU is used for medical emergencies and for patients whose condition can deteriorate quickly:

Cardiac events

Chest pain, arrhythmia and heart failure, with ECG and echocardiography available on site.

Respiratory failure

Severe pneumonia, acute asthma and COPD needing oxygen or ventilator support.

Neurological emergencies

Paralysis and brain haemorrhage requiring close neurological observation.

Poisoning and snake bite

Cases needing airway protection and continuous monitoring. More on this.

Severe infections

Severe malaria, dengue and jaundice with systemic complications.

Diabetic emergencies

Decompensated diabetes needing stabilisation and close monitoring.

Who looks after the unit

Dr. Ambarish A. Panchasara, MBBS, MD (Medicine), is the consulting physician. He trained at B. J. Medical College, Ahmedabad, and is registered with the Gujarat Medical Council under registration number G-48998.

Nursing staff are present at the hospital through the night, with the doctor on call. Consultations are available in Gujarati, Hindi and English.

The hospital does not operate its own ambulance. Please arrange local transport, and call so staff can be ready when you arrive.
